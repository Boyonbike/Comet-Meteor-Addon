# Highway Fly

[Documentation index](../README.md) · [Project README](../../README.md)

Module: **highway-fly**.

**highway-fly** in the **Comet** category coordinates highway flight with three independent modules: **Anti Portal**, **Auto Set Home** and **Auto Lobby**. Its **Integrations** section requests each helper during trips; these switches remain on by default. Configure each helper in its own module, using the settings shortcuts on Highway Fly's screen.

A trip-managed helper shows as enabled, but only its independent activation is saved. Stopping Highway Fly releases its requests and preserves helpers you enabled independently. Use **Keep enabled independently** on a managed helper to keep it running after the trip. Explicitly disabling a managed helper also turns off its Highway Fly integration switch.

Highway Fly's settings are ordered **Route**, **Flight & Altitude**, **Navigation & Recovery**, **Elytra & Safety**, **Integrations**, **Camera & Controls**, **Webhooks** and **Diagnostics**. Existing profiles migrate helper settings, integration choices and keybinds, and the old **Dashboard** section moves to [Flight Dashboard](flight-dashboard.md); explicit settings in independent modules take precedence.

Set the home name and destination before starting. The flight follows a locked straight line. Set **destination** to a point down the highway, set **target-y** to the altitude you want, and turn it on with an elytra worn, or let **swap-elytra** put one on from your inventory. It takes off from flat ground, climbs vertically to **target-y**, holds the line and altitude, and lands at the destination so you can `/tpa` to the account later. Flight uses a small local corridor pathfinder and no fireworks; Baritone handles walking recovery around lava and blocked stretches.

- **lock-altitude** (default on) holds **target-y**. Turn it off to hold the destination block's Y. **min-y** and **max-y** (defaults 5 and 125) are the band a path may use. **vertical-step** is the fallback altitude step for the simple detour. **detour-width** (default 1.2) is the total clear width the fallback probe requires; 0.6 is the player's own body, so 1.2 keeps 0.3 blocks clear on each side.
- **speed-bps** (default **60**), **vertical-speed-bps** (default 60), **lookahead** (default 48), **probe-distance** (default 48) and **turn-rate** (default 15 degrees per tick) tune the flight. 60 b/s leaves the server time to load chunks; the module waits at unloaded chunks instead of loading them. The controller uses Adaptive Elytra Fly's movement engine and its ramp settings, but Adaptive Elytra Fly itself must be off. Highway Fly owns its terrain lookahead, 0.03-block stopping margin and turn rate; Adaptive Elytra Fly's close-object crawl, collision release and maneuver cap do not override its navigation. Physical collision, lava and loaded-chunk checks remain active.
- **pathfind** (default on), **corridor-width** (default **5**) and **corridor-height** (default **3**) make a local grid across the highway. The grid is aligned with the flight line and checks the player's real body with swept corridor probes; an unsuccessful coarse search retries with quarter-block lateral and vertical samples within the same physical corridor, with bounded search and probe work; it can use a five-wide, three-tall cross-section. In elytra flight the body is only 0.6 blocks tall, so it can thread a one-block-by-one-block gap; the test covers that flying body and a diagonal wall. Set the width/height to the actual highway interior. It does not load chunks and only runs when the direct line is blocked.
- **path-speed-bps** (default **8**) is the speed cap while following a path through a tight gap; the normal **speed-bps** resumes once the direct line is clear. A one-block gap needs alignment time, so lowering this is safer than trying to thread it at cruise speed.
- On a clear line it flies straight ahead at the chosen altitude. At activation and after each Baritone recovery walk, the module scans the loaded free cross-section around the player and re-anchors the flight line to its middle, so a start or recovery that is off-center does not fly parallel to the highway; Baritone walking targets use a stable standard axis or diagonal through the configured destination, chosen once from the route heading. Your current position is projected onto that line, then the target is placed **walk-distance** blocks ahead along it, capped at the destination. Local side offsets do not shift this line. Only clear standing blocks on that same line may become walking endpoints; retries move farther along the line. Exact block goals prevent Baritone stopping in an adjacent lane. The scan uses the real player body and never loads chunks. When the direct corridor is blocked, the pathfinder searches the configured cross-section for a route through the full local horizon, keeps that path while it remains usable, and follows monotonic waypoints with an eight-block local lookahead. It can align sideways or vertically before advancing past a nearby obstacle, follows the actual waypoint slope, and caps both horizontal and vertical speed on tight paths. It replans only when the committed path is blocked or exhausted, requires six clear ticks before returning to cruise, and briefly slows after a server correction. Steering changes are smoothed once per movement update. Upcoming bends, turn rate, braking distance and local clearance set the speed budget; open sections accelerate again after leaving a tight approach. Failed or server-rejected approaches are avoided locally for ten seconds, with at most eight remembered positions. A partial route ending at a known obstacle is rejected and handed to Baritone immediately. A partial route limited by unloaded chunks may advance safely before waiting. Turning **pathfind** off retains the older yaw/altitude detour. The pathfinder is a short-horizon local planner, not a global route follower, so it still cannot turn a highway corner.
- **portal-mode** controls Nether portals on the line. **Avoid** (default) treats a portal as an obstacle and detours around it. **Pass** treats it as open and keeps flying; it will not begin a landing while standing in a portal. **Stop** stops before the first portal within **portal-distance** (default 16). A dimension change always stops the flight. Passing a portal at speed is best-effort; the server decides whether the transition happens.
- **auto-takeoff** (default on) jumps, opens the elytra from flat ground, then climbs vertically to **target-y** before cruising forward. If the ceiling blocks the climb, it warns once and continues at its current altitude instead of stopping; it can climb again later. If a takeoff attempt fails to open the elytra, the same Baritone walk hand-off runs before another takeoff; this applies on a normal start and when lobby recovery resumes the flight. **recover-from-ground** (default on) takes off again if the flight touches the ground before the destination; it does not treat that as the end of the trip. **land-on-arrival** (default on) descends only at the destination; turn it off to stop in the air there. **min-durability** (default 10) is when auto-swap puts in a better spare; without one it logs out before the worn elytra breaks. Durability protection stays active during landing until confirmed touchdown. Turning **swap-elytra** off disables automatic swapping, while low durability still causes a protective logout. **stop-before-lava** (default on) first lets the airborne pathfinder and detour planner find a safe flight over or around lava. Only when flight cannot pass does it land and ask Baritone to route around the lava before taking off again. Lava is treated as an obstacle by every flight probe, including during descent. Safe overflight remains available with **stop-before-lava** on. Fluid checks use the actual lava surface, including shallow flowing lava, rather than treating the whole block as full. Predicted lava contact can stop or replan a flight move but never directly causes a lava logout. During every recovery walk, Baritone temporarily has walking on lava and parkour disabled and strict liquid checks enabled; its previous settings are restored when walking stops. If Baritone is missing or disabled, it holds and retries; the no-progress watchdog can still end a genuinely blocked trip. **pass-end-crystals** (default on) removes end crystal collision boxes from the movement solve so a crystal on the line does not stop the flight; their explosion damage is still real. **walk-fallback** (default on) hands off to Baritone after one failed elytra path attempt; the old configurable three-attempt loop has been removed, including for saved profiles. If the line stays blocked, it lands, asks Baritone to walk **walk-distance** (default 20) blocks ahead along the route, then takes off and resumes the line. The walk target is projected onto the destination’s standard highway line, then resolved to a loaded, clear standing position on that line near the actual ground level after landing. It searches up to 32 blocks farther for clear jump/opening headroom and a six-block flying departure, capped at 200 blocks and the destination; it does not ask Baritone to stand at an unreachable flight altitude. The local pathfinder also keeps vertical path points within half the configured **corridor-height** of **target-y**. **rubberband-detection** (default on) counts server position corrections within **rubberband-window-ticks** (default 60); after **rubberband-threshold** (default 2) it uses the same landing hand-off with **rubberband-walk-distance** (default 10). Baritone must be installed for walking recovery. Once it takes over, it owns normal movement, step-up and intermediate route heights; flight prediction cannot interrupt its detour. The landing wait is bounded to 30 seconds, while a walking route may take longer as long as it progresses. Failed walking targets retry 20 blocks farther ahead, capped at 200 blocks. After eight failed recoveries, or failure to land for recovery within 30 seconds, Highway Fly logs out deliberately rather than looping indefinitely. A completed walk resets the failure counter. **no-progress-timeout** (default **30 seconds**) watches takeoff, blocked flight, chunk waits and stalled walking. Flight requires a new best distance at least one block closer to the destination. Walking instead requires advancement through Baritone’s current path, so valid sideways/backwards/up/down lava detours can continue. Recalculating a path or oscillating between already completed movements does not restart the timer. Active eating during walking recovery grants up to five seconds of extra stall time per no-progress period; it does not reset the time already stalled. Cruise, path following, detours and blocked flight hand off to Baritone after **five seconds** without a new best distance; takeoff, climbing and chunk waits retain the configured timeout. A stalled flight gets a Baritone attempt; a stalled recovery logs out.
- **minimum-health** (default **8 health points**, or **4 red hearts**) logs out when the latest server-reported remaining health is at or below this threshold during a trip, including walking and lobby recovery. Healing restores the health available to this check; cumulative trip damage shown on the dashboard is informational and cannot trigger a logout. Golden absorption hearts are excluded from this threshold, so their expiry cannot count as damage or cause a false logout. Set **0** to disable the low-health check. A lava-triggered logout requires the player to actually be in lava; lava near/below the highway, a predicted movement, or Baritone’s chosen route height cannot trigger it. Actual immersion logs out immediately. Safety logouts cancel saved trip recovery and disable Meteor AutoReconnect.
- Eating is managed separately: enable Meteor **AutoGap** and keep golden or enchanted golden apples in the hotbar; AutoGap does not search the main inventory or offhand. Meteor **AutoEat** blacklists both apple types by default. AutoGap's own health threshold includes absorption, and its default settings pause Baritone and combat auras while eating. Highway Fly does not enable or configure these modules or manage combat against mobs during walking recovery.
- **swap-elytra** (default on) puts the most durable elytra in your hotbar, main inventory or offhand on when the flight starts without one, and swaps in a better spare when the worn elytra reaches **min-durability**. The displaced item returns to the spare's slot, so a full inventory still works; broken elytra and Curse of Binding are skipped. If low durability is reached while a container or held cursor item blocks a safe swap, Highway Fly logs out rather than letting the wings fail. If no usable spare above **min-durability** is left, it disconnects before the worn elytra breaks and turns off Meteor's **AutoReconnect**, so a reconnect does not put you back in the air. **swap-back** (default on) puts the chestplate the elytra replaced back on once the flight ends on the ground; the restore also waits for a landing or a free inventory. With both **swap-elytra** and **swap-back** enabled, grounded Baritone walking and lobby recovery also equip an available chestplate from your inventory. A usable elytra is equipped again before takeoff. Turning either option off disables this recovery armour handoff.
- **free-look** (default on) lets the mouse turn the camera while airborne without changing the player's flight heading or the autopilot's movement direction. The player body and server receive the actual autopilot yaw and pitch, including turns and climbs; only the rendered camera moves. Mouse buttons, scroll and GUI mouse use are unaffected. With **free-look** off, **disable-mouse-look** (default on) keeps the old behavior: mouse look is ignored only while the elytra is flying, so the controller owns the camera. Camera-only free look is a view feature, not a steering override; if the view is turned and the flight is still moving, that is expected.
- It refuses to start if another flight controller is active: **baritone-elytra**, **auto-wasp-devils**, **auto-walk**, Meteor's **Elytra Fly**, or **adaptive-elytra-fly**. It also refuses in water, lava, a vehicle or Freecam, and needs a new destination at least 64 blocks away. An existing saved trip can resume closer to its destination so it can finish landing. Without **swap-elytra** it needs an elytra worn before it starts; with it, it needs a usable elytra in the inventory.
- **It is a straight-line controller, not a highway follower.** It follows the line between the activation point and destination; it does not read the highway, plan a route or turn corners. Start it lined up with the highway. A curve or a long diagonal section needs a new destination; otherwise it will hold the original line and detour or stop.
- **No in-game verification yet.** The obstacle probes, altitude band, local pathfinder, 1×1 fall-flying body gap, detours and portal handling are unit-tested, but live server movement checks, setbacks at 60 b/s and 6b6t's Nether highway behaviour still need gameplay testing. Lower **speed-bps** or **path-speed-bps** further if corrections are frequent.

The module list shows `Taking off`, `Climbing`, remaining blocks, `Detour` or `Path`, `Chunks`, `Blocked`, `Landing` or `Done`.

## Webhooks

Highway Fly can post one Discord message for the events you would otherwise miss while flying or away. Enable **webhook** and paste a Discord channel webhook URL into **webhook-url**; the module sends through that one URL and uses a different title, colour and description for each event. Trip events are never posted when the switch is off or the URL is blank. The module screen has **Send test webhook**, which posts one manual test with the current URL without waiting for a trip event or changing your event switches.

- Sent events include a route start or resume, a dimension change, death, arrival or another route stop, a lava handoff, a deliberate safety logout (low health, lava, no progress, failed recovery or low durability), an unexpected disconnect, lobby recovery starting or waiting, a failed cracked-account login, portal discovery and portal waiting, and conditions that need you to step in (landing, leaving a vehicle, a stuck walk, an unloadable portal exit, or a portal that will not transport).
- Each confirmed home replacement sends one check-in when **home-replaced-events** is enabled, including home, replacement coordinates/dimension, confirmation time, health and route progress. This replaces the periodic status timer. Duplicate success replies do not create extra check-ins; saved history is never replayed to Discord after restart. The old **periodic-status** preference migrates to this switch. Death emits one alert and ends the trip before any further movement.
- Every message uses the same layout: a short title, the reason, the player name and head, server address, dimension and block position, plus route fields such as destination or remaining distance where they apply.
- **mention** (optional) pings one Discord user or role on **needs attention** and **safety logout** messages only. Paste a raw numeric ID or a copied `<@...>` / `<@&...>` mention; anything else is ignored, and other events never ping. Leave it blank for no pings.
- **Webhook Timing → event-cooldown-seconds** (default 30, 0 disables) sets the minimum gap between repeats of the same event. Confirmed home replacements, death and safety logout are exempt. The periodic status interval has been removed.
- The message is built on the game thread and posted asynchronously, so a slow connection does not stall the flight. HTTP failures are logged to the Meteor log and never stop the trip.
- The URL and messages are stored in plain text in the Meteor config, and the messages contain your server address and coordinates. Only Discord webhook URLs are accepted. Treat the URL like a password; anyone who has it can post in that channel.

The payload shape, escaping and URL validation are unit-tested. Live delivery to a Discord channel still needs a gameplay check.

Webhook delivery uses a shared asynchronous queue for Highway Fly, Call to Arms and Stash Report. Requests ask Discord to confirm the saved message. Network failures and server errors retry with exponential backoff; rate limits honor Discord's retry delay, including global limits. The queue is bounded to 32 messages, 8 MiB per message, five attempts and two minutes of retention. Invalid or deleted webhook endpoints (HTTP 401/403/404) are suppressed for the rest of the process, within a bounded endpoint cache; use a new valid URL or restart after repairing permissions. Pending messages are memory-only and do not survive closing the client. An ambiguous timeout can produce a duplicate when retried.

Manual module stops emit one final notification before clearing the trip; arrival, safety logout and recovery completion suppress duplicate stop messages. Actual disconnect alerts distinguish manual exits from unexpected losses, and ordinary server reconfiguration/transfers do not report a lost connection.
